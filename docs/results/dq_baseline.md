## 4. Schema and vocabulary heterogeneity

- **Sex coding:** WHO uses ['SEX_BTSX', 'SEX_FMLE', 'SEX_MLE']; UNICEF uses ['F', 'M', '_T']. The same idea ('both sexes') has different codes.
- **Wealth quintile coding:** WHO uses ['WEALTHQUINTILE_TOTL', 'WEALTHQUINTILE_WQ1', 'WEALTHQUINTILE_WQ2', 'WEALTHQUINTILE_WQ3', 'WEALTHQUINTILE_WQ4', 'WEALTHQUINTILE_WQ5']; UNICEF uses ['B20', 'B40', 'B60', 'B80', 'Q1', 'Q2', 'Q3', 'Q4', 'Q5', 'R20', 'R40', 'R60', 'R80', '_T'].
- **Regional identifiers:** 7 of 54 African states (13.0%) are *not* in WHO's `AFR` region: {'EMR': ['DJI', 'EGY', 'LBY', 'MAR', 'SDN', 'SOM', 'TUN']}. Selecting 'Africa' by WHO region would silently drop them.
- **UNICEF schemas:** 4 different column sets across 7 series; only 18.4% of all columns appear in every series.
- **Identifiers:** WHO and UNICEF carry ISO3 codes; the World Bank carries both ISO2 (`country_id`) and ISO3 (`countryiso3code`) plus a free-text country name.

## 5. Method and limitations

- Measured on the *staging* layer: typed but not cleaned, deduplicated or harmonized. This is the baseline against which the integrated layer will be compared.
- *Invalid* means any of: unparseable value text, value outside a plausible range per concept (e.g. 0 to 100 for percentages), value outside the source's own uncertainty bounds, year outside the snapshot's scope, or a country code outside the 54 states.
- High validity does not mean the data is *correct*; it means it passes these plausibility checks. Cross-source accuracy is assessed after harmonization.
- Outlier detection and cross-source value agreement are not measured here, because they are only meaningful once disaggregation is resolved.
- Plausible ranges are judgements recorded in `EXPECTED_RANGES` (`src/healthbridge/dq/baseline.py`).
